Script: Here is a ten-second trick to test if your remote batteries are actually dead. Grab your TV remote and open the camera app on your phone. If you see a pulsing purple glow on your screen, the batteries are fine and your TV is the issue.

Script: The computer that put humanity on the moon had less memory than a single digital photo. The Apollo Guidance Computer in nineteen sixty-nine operated on just four kilobytes of RAM and about seventy-two kilobytes of ROM. For perspective, the smartphone in your pocket has millions of times more computing power. Astronauts literally navigated across space on memory cables woven by hand through tiny magnetic rings.

Script: Have you ever walked into a room and instantly forgotten why you went there? Scientists call this the Doorway Effect, and it is a real psychological phenomenon. When you walk through a door, your mind clears its working memory to prepare for the new environment.

VIDEO 6: Underwater Rivers
Script: Deep beneath the ocean surface, there are actual flowing rivers with their own shorelines. In underwater caves like Cenote Angelita in Mexico, dense layers of hydrogen sulfide separate fresh water above from heavy salt water below. This creates a distinct visual barrier that behaves like an underwater river, complete with fallen trees lining the banks. Scuba divers can literally float along the edge of a river sixty feet underwater.

VIDEO 8: Origin of Cliché
Script: The word cliché was originally an onomatopoeia for a heavy metal sound. In nineteenth-century French print shops, printers created molded metal casting plates to duplicate frequently used phrases without reassembling each tiny letter. The cast made a sharp clicking sound as it stamped the plate, which sounded like the word cliché. Over time, that repetitive mechanical sound became our term for an overused expression.
Caption: Every cliché literally started as an industrial sound effect. 🖨️🔤

VIDEO 9: Immortal Honey
Script: Archaeologists have found pots of honey in ancient Egyptian tombs that are still perfectly edible. Honey is naturally immortal due to its unique chemistry. It contains almost zero water, carries a high acid level, and naturally produces hydrogen peroxide thanks to an enzyme in bee stomachs. Because of this inhospitable environment, bacteria and mold cannot survive inside it, meaning genuine honey never technically spoils.
Caption: Pure honey basically ignores the concept of time. 🍯🐝
