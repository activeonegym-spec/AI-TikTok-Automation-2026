VIDEO 1: Banana Radiation
Script: Did you know you eat radioactive material every single week? Bananas naturally contain potassium, and a tiny percentage of all potassium on Earth is the radioactive isotope potassium-40. Don't panic, though. You would have to eat roughly ten million bananas in one sitting to experience acute radiation sickness. So keep enjoying your morning smoothie safely.

VIDEO 2: Remove Sticky Residue
Script: Stop scratching stubborn price tags with your fingernails. When you peel off a label and it leaves behind that gross, sticky goo, just head to your kitchen pantry. Put a tiny drop of regular cooking oil directly on the residue, wait two minutes, and wipe it clean with a paper towel. The oil dissolves the adhesive bonds without scratching the surface.

Script: If you anger a crow, it might hold a grudge against you forever. Researchers tested crow memory using rubber masks and discovered something wild. Even crazier, they warn their flock and their offspring, passing the grudge down across generations.

VIDEO 4: Airplane Wi-Fi Secrets
Script: Ever wonder how your phone gets high-speed internet at 35,000 feet? Airplanes use two main systems to get you online. When flying over land, antennas beneath the fuselage connect to specialized cell towers on the ground. When cruising across oceans, a dome antenna on top of the plane tracks satellites orbiting thousands of miles above Earth in real time.
Caption: How in-flight internet actually works ✈️

VIDEO 6: The Doorway Effect
Script: You walk into a room, stop, and instantly forget why you're there. Psychologists call this the Doorway Effect. Walking through a physical threshold acts as an event boundary in your brain. Your mind files away thoughts from the previous room to make space for the new environment, accidentally deleting your mental reminder to grab your car keys.

Petrov trusted his intuition that it was a computer glitch and refused to launch. His judgment call was right, saving billions of lives.

VIDEO 9: The McGurk Illusion
Script: Your eyes can actually change what your ears hear. In an experiment called the McGurk effect, scientists play audio of a voice saying 'ba', while showing a video of lips mouthing 'ga'. When you watch the mouth move, your brain blends the sensory input and you hear 'da'. But if you close your eyes, the sound immediately switches back to 'ba'.
Caption: Try this mind trick right now! 🤯
